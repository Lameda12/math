An AI model just claimed 372 new results in mathematics.
722 papers. 34,815 pages. Nobody is going to read all of it.

So I built a way not to.

Every researcher asks the same first question about AI-generated math: which of these can I trust? The Proposition starts there.

→ Every result is tagged by evidence. 235 have a Lean formalization of at least part of the claim, 6 ship code that reproduces their computations, 131 are paper only.
→ Pick your field (17, with arXiv codes). One card per result: the claim first, the exact Lean theorem one keystroke away, then the papers with BibTeX.
→ Judge each one in seconds with keys 1 to 4: read it, doubt it, not new, not my area.
→ You only see how other researchers judged a result after you judge it yourself. Peer review hides the other referees for the same reason.
→ Leave with a reading list and its BibTeX.

None of these results are refereed. The tool doesn't tell you what to believe. It tells you where to look first.

If you work in number theory, complexity, combinatorics or any of the other 14 fields, I'd love to see what you flag as "doubt it."
